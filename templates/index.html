How does it work?

CAPS

The CAPS (Cleaved Amplified Polymorphic Sequence) technique exploits a natural variation in the DNA (a polymorphism) that either creates or destroys a cutting site for a restriction enzyme. If the site is present, the DNA is cut.

The EasyCAPS Finder compares the two alleles you provide, and if any specific CAPS sites are found in either sequence, they will be displayed in the results.

dCAPS

The dCAPS (derived CAPS) technique intentionally introduces a mismatch in the PCR primer to artificially create a restriction site in only one of the alleles.

The EasyCAPS Finder designs potential dCAPS primers with 1, 2, or 3 mismatches that allow for differentiation between the alleles.

Direct CRISPR PAM Modification



How does it work?

Two-Step Strategy

Allele conversion is performed in two sequential transformations to prevent re-cleavage of the edited allele. First, gRNA1 directs the insertion of a donor cassette that replaces the original target site with a new recognition sequence for gRNA2. In a second step, gRNA2 is used to introduce the desired variant, generating allele 2.

Direct CRISPR Strategy

The replacement of alleles is achieved in a single transformation step. A repair template carries a silent base mutation within the protospacer adjacent motif (PAM). This strategy masks the Cas9 recognition site in the new allele, preventing recurrent cleavage and ensuring the efficiency of the edit without altering the protein sequence.

The EasyCAPS Finder translates allele 1 and identifies the PAMs related to the gRNAs provided by the user. The tool delivers modifications in the PAM sequence that do not alter the original protein, allowing for direct CRISPR.
